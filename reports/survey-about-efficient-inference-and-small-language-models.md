# Survey on Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
- Efficient inference techniques for small language models include model compression methods such as distillation and quantization [1].
- Small language models demonstrate competitive efficiency and are notably more energy-efficient compared to large language models in various applications [2].
- Diverse benchmarks evaluate inference efficiency using parameters like throughput, energy consumption, and latency [3].
- Future trends indicate increased efficiency of small language models, though challenges such as generalization remain [4].

## Background
Efficient inference and optimization of small language models (SLMs) are crucial in the context of deploying language technologies in real-world applications. The advancements observed in SLMs through optimization techniques facilitate improved performance while reducing operational burdens.

## Current Techniques for Optimizing Inference
Current techniques for optimizing inference in SLMs encompass model compression methods such as distillation and quantization. These techniques effectively reduce the model size and resource consumption while maintaining performance. Approaches including efficient routing frameworks for multi-agent systems and strategies involving parallel processing across GPUs enhance computational efficiency [1].

## Comparison of Small and Large Language Models
While large language models (LLMs) often excel in raw performance, research suggests that SLMs can compete effectively in efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Integrative frameworks leveraging the strengths of both model types demonstrate that SLMs are significantly more energy-efficient in various applications such as code generation and malware detection [2].

## Benchmarks and Metrics
A variety of benchmarks are employed to evaluate inference efficiency in language models, emphasizing parameters such as throughput, energy consumption, latency, and cost per response. Innovative frameworks like AHELM and VHELM propose standardized evaluation methodologies tailored to model characteristics and real-world applications, responding to the industry's need for effective performance assessment [3].

## Trends and Open Problems
Looking towards the future, SLMs reveal a trend towards increased efficiency and reduced operational costs. Predictions indicate that SLMs can serve responses at a cost substantially lower than LLMs; however, challenges like generalization, data scarcity, and biases within existing benchmarks create barriers that must be addressed for wider adoption and optimization [4].
